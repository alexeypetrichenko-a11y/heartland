# MIL-00-DESIGN · Strategic land combat

**Owner decision recorded.** Model B and its initial calibration are approved. The safeguards below propose deterministic initial policies for review. Production gameplay remains outside this PR. Baseline main is `0894d8213ac06b3ed418b7cf4b12ce68c2bdbc15`. MIL-01A remains separate in PR #39.

## Existing architecture and accepted baseline

- `src/game/military.ts` resolves player orders by division ID and allocates defender losses by ID. Each attacker and its defenders calculate damage from pre-round strength, then the next attacker sees the modified state. The provisional damage rules are half attacker strength and one-quarter defender strength with integer rounding. An attack persists until capture, destruction, replacement or invalidation. Capture requires surviving attackers and complete depletion of the garrison. WORLD-04 reproduced a lone attacker losing 75% in the first week against three full divisions. [WORLD-04 combat study](../world/WORLD-04-COMBAT-STUDY.md)
- `advanceTurn.ts` validates and applies commands before military resolution. `economyBridge.ts` routes the turn through `advanceWeekTogether`, settling economic occupation from the resulting regional controller while retaining legal ownership. `saveFormat.ts` and `saveValidation.ts` verify the checksummed checkpoint against the scenario fixture and economy. The current Military overview surfaces continuing orders and the weekly report lists completed results. [WORLD-04 iPhone corrections](../world/WORLD-04-IPHONE-CORRECTIONS.md)
- The owner accepted WORLD-04-FINAL on iPhone SE (2022), iOS 26.2, and approved two physical benchmark runs. PRs #36 and #38 are merged. Each benchmark ran 156 turns, 61 economic zones and 90 divisions.

## Alternatives

| Criterion | A · Strength and retreat | B · Coordinated engagement and progress | C · Readiness, equipment and logistics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic depth | Commit manpower and choose when to leave | Commit across regions, reinforce and manage persistent pressure | Multiple resource and readiness tradeoffs |
| Balance | Few knobs, strong dependence on headcount | Attrition, tempo and terrain can be tuned separately | More interactions to calibrate, with risk of runaway advantages |
| Predictability | High with known strength | Visible trend and bounded losses, with uncertain enemy strength | More hidden inputs and harder attribution |
| Mobile interface | A single comparison and retreat button | One engagement card with detail on demand | Several resource and readiness indicators |
| AI | Ratio gate and fallback | Ratio bands, reliable fallback and reserve decisions | Cross-front resource allocation and longer lookahead |
| Multiplayer determinism | Simple with snapshot aggregation | Explicit conflict policy, fixed arithmetic and atomic settlement | More allocation and dependency order to specify |
| Saves | Rules version and existing standing orders | Add persistent engagements and their participation/outcomes | Track supplies, equipment and readiness over time |
| Performance | Linear scan of participating divisions | Indexed regional groups and local edge checks | Potential route and logistics recomputation |
| Naval and amphibious extension | Separate progress and arrival semantics would be needed | Contested arrival can feed an engagement from a future beachhead | Rich integration once naval and logistics systems exist |

**Approved: B.** Engagements lasting several weeks give players time to respond without issuing the same order every turn. Use personnel equivalents as the only division strength input in the first phase, with 25,000 as full strength. Show effectiveness as the remaining share of full strength and use operational statuses to convey when a unit cannot fight. Separate morale and equipment would increase save and interface complexity before testing has identified a need. In a 156-week match that lasts two hours, each week gets about 46 seconds on average. Keep key decisions on the overview and require a tap only to change an order or see details.

## Approved rules and proposed settlement details

1. A land assault groups eligible attacks against a region by conflict side. Track one engagement at that target against its current controller. Store each attacker's origin and aggregate all participating divisions before calculating anything. Apply losses once per division from the same snapshot. Incoming orders from several origins add force with no initial direction bonus. Reinforcements join in their eligible settlement week. They bring strength and increase that side's committed-strength baseline by their joining strength, with no reset to progress. A departure removes that participant's baseline contribution and surviving strength. A unit rejoining contributes its retained readiness baseline; fresh eligible units contribute their strength at commitment. An individual at or below its readiness floor cannot volunteer for a new attack or reinforcement even when the combined force is above its side threshold.
2. Use integer arithmetic. Start progress at zero and bound it to −100 through +100. Let A and D be the participating sums after bounded modifiers. Start with D = floor(defender strength × 115 ÷ 100). Progress changes by the sign of A−D times floor(45 × |A−D| ÷ (A+D)). Handle an empty side before doing any division. Each unit loses the floor of its manpower times its side’s loss rate. In basis points the rates are min(600, floor(300 × D ÷ A)) for attackers and min(600, floor(300 × A ÷ D)) for defenders. Round per unit without redistributing remainders. Check multiplication bounds and ensure zero ordinary loss remains possible at very small strengths. An uncontested eligible land advance may capture in the same settlement.
3. An attacker is **defeated** when stopped at −100 progress or at or below 80% of committed strength. Its surviving divisions retreat toward their eligible origin first, or another eligible friendly fallback if necessary. Defenders at +100 progress or at or below 80% attempt **retreat** to a friendly-controlled, uncontested adjacent region reachable by military land edges at settlement. Voluntary disengagement uses the same eligibility checks and applies before losses for the week. A player can name a preferred eligible fallback. With several eligible fallbacks and no usable preference, choose by an explicit map policy, such as shortest distance to a friendly reserve and a stable region key for equal candidates. A retreat or successful voluntary combat withdrawal in week W prevents new commitments in week W+1. Earliest recommitment is W+2, subject to readiness. A threatened recovering division can still be present as a defender.
4. A division reaching the loss threshold has reduced **combat effectiveness** and may be repelled with personnel remaining. If the origin is no longer usable or a defender's fallback is blocked by another engagement, keep the formation pinned, recheck next week and expose the reason. Confirm **encirclement** only if there is no eligible friendly land exit structurally at settlement. The candidate surrender rule requires two consecutive qualifying weeks while encircled and facing a breakthrough or low strength. A successful exit or loss of the qualifying condition resets the count. **Surrender** ends the formation's participation and records surviving personnel as captured separately from casualties. **Destruction** would mean the formation and surviving personnel are lost as an explicit exceptional outcome. Leave it disabled in the first implementation. Normal weekly combat cannot cause it. Apply both rules symmetrically to attackers and defenders. Evaluate both sides against the same post-loss snapshot. If both qualify and exit, neither captures. A sole remaining attacker must still be eligible to occupy.
5. Capture after defenders withdraw, surrender or are absent requires an eligible surviving attacking occupation force. Keep ownership unchanged while defenders are pinned or while claimants dispute the result. On capture, place the winning participating force in the province, end those commitments and emit one control change. Defeat or a voluntary attacker withdrawal without a capture leaves territorial control unchanged. Failed voluntary withdrawal should produce a report entry and show the unit's current commitment.

### Concurrent fronts and determinism

Freeze diplomacy, controllers, orders and locations before assigning any participant. Apply one canonical operation per division per week. A rejected bundle leaves the existing drafts and reservations intact. Resolve in this order.

1. Reserve eligible reinforcements and ongoing commitments first, after applying approved withdrawals. Resolve new exact reverse land orders on one edge as a pair. Compare the unmodified manpower of the two committed groups. If one side has less than 25% of the other, open the stronger side’s assault and treat the weaker group as local defenders. A smaller reverse order cannot delay it. At or above 25%, combine both groups into one field clash without the regional defensive bonus. Calculate losses from one snapshot. Both sides retain their origin unless they retreat. The clash itself transfers no territory. End it after at most eight combat rounds, including when rounding makes casualties and progress zero. If a retreat-qualified formation has no usable exit, resolve the pinned or surrender process first. An eligible follow-up assault requires a later order. Opposing orders resolving to a clash cease when it ends. The harness exercises the equal clash and boundary values.
2. Assign remaining valid outgoing units to their declared operation. A nonreciprocal incoming attack does not hold those units. It encounters the remaining eligible garrison and can exploit an undefended origin, including a cross-capture. Use frozen origins for commitments and validate surviving destinations together before applying proposed captures. Convergent unreserved claims belonging to the same side aggregate. Reserve each unit once, then allocate unreserved defending units by their current region. If unrelated sides contest a target, defer their assaults and report the contention. No coalition or winner is inferred from submission order.
3. Persist each division’s readiness baseline across pauses, withdrawals, assignments and saves. Ordinary retreat changes eligibility and location without restoring personnel or lowering that baseline. A later authorized recovery or replenishment system can change it explicitly. A force with mixed veteran and fresh divisions keeps its signed progress and prior losses. Additions change the committed totals for future threshold checks. Retain each active front’s progress across a blocked-withdrawal suspension. No cancellation or stage action rewinds the last settled state.
4. Distinguish structural encirclement from a temporarily denied fallback. Count qualifying encirclement weeks consecutively and clear the streak when the conditions cease. A structural encirclement surrenders on the second qualifying week. After three consecutive retreat-qualified weeks with no usable route, regardless of changes in the denial reason, suspend settlement on that front and report the blocked result. Keep ownership and surviving personnel in place, end automatic attack orders, and retain the front checkpoint. A reopened exit takes priority over a new order. Require one full week with no rematch on that front and a now-available exit before renewed orders reopen the suspended fight. While suspended, reevaluate exits weekly without charging ordinary combat losses. Two consecutive qualifying weeks of structural encirclement can still resolve surrender. Use the same policy for either side.

These policies trade some tactical freedom for predictable settlement. The screening threshold produces a discontinuity at 25%. A field clash can cost both forces time and manpower without any control change. Outgoing orders can expose their origin. Temporary suspension can delay a capture and creates an incentive to keep an exit contested elsewhere. Test those effects in short matches before adopting the proposed gate and suspension window. Region identity may break a genuine fallback-route tie; division identity and submission order never decide arithmetic, claims or casualties.

Existing diplomacy supplies the first eligibility checks. Future war and alliance rules can supply explicit attack, defence and access permissions at the same boundary. Recheck fallback against contested regions and proposed captures before committing. Ordinary friendly movement becomes available for combat the following week; eligible named reinforcements join the current settlement.

### Collective orders

The map and Military menu share one selection model. A collective draft contains action, selected division IDs, destination or engagement where applicable, intended execution week and optional fallback. Support **Attack**, **Move**, **Defend**, **Reinforce** and **Disengage / retreat**. Attack accepts several origins when each selected division is individually eligible. Preview displays selected count, summed known friendly strength, all origin regions, destination or engagement, execution week and eligibility restrictions. Show per-division reasons for failure and leave the selection editable.

Validate the complete bundle before staging and again against the frozen weekly state at settlement. Reject atomically when any member is ineligible. A rejected staged bundle reports the reason and leaves pre-existing orders in place. Accepting a bundle explicitly replaces intersecting orders as a whole, after showing affected drafts and requiring confirmation. Complete validation and normalization produce one authoritative intent per division. A successful attack bundle can join compatible orders in the same engagement while retaining individual origin and strength. Save the bundle identity and pending membership so a restored draft can still be canceled or replaced as a unit.

## State, APIs and system boundaries

- Add a pinned match-level combat rules ID, with `legacy-t07` and a candidate `strategic-land-v1`. Add a versioned engagement record with target, defender/controller, conflict side, start and last settled week, phase, signed progress, participants and origins, committed strength per participant, withdrawal recovery eligibility, suspension week and cause, consecutive blocked and encircled counts. Keep manpower, retained readiness baseline and next eligible commitment week on each division and its commitment singular. Preserve suspended front state across saves. Save pending per-seat orders and relevant completed results, with deterministic event keys and bounded report history. Closing an engagement clears its active references and writes a typed outcome.
- Extend commands additively with commitment, reinforcement and disengagement intents plus expected execution week and optional fallback. A pure resolver takes the frozen snapshot and returns proposed unit, engagement and control updates plus report events. Validate control, unique use of each division, eligibility and ownership together before producing the in-memory commit. Feed only final control changes into atomic economy settlement. A rejected economy or game turn keeps the previous reports, save and drafts intact.
- Keep the existing save format decoder and scenario/economy fingerprint checks. A missing combat rules ID means legacy T07 on both fictional and exact-version European saves, including mid-attack sessions. Only new matches offered the new rules select them at creation. Preserve legacy standing orders, economic values and outcomes on replay. Keep immutable scenario packages byte-for-byte. Add a versioned SAVE-01 strategy checkpoint payload with discriminated validation for rules, bounded values, references, phases, drafts, ownership, week and engagement consistency. Reject unsupported rules or incompatible versions without replacing the existing save. Any future conversion requires an explicit owner decision and separate slot or export. Preserve transaction receipt timing, stale-tab protection and review origin isolation.
- For two to four human seats, normalize validated private submissions into one immutable weekly bundle. A future authoritative settler applies it once per week and makes retries idempotent. Resolve simultaneous diplomacy before freezing eligibility, then settle military, control and economy atomically. The local player uses the same command contract. Start AI with conservative risk bands, a reserve and fallback check, and deterministic reinforcement and stop thresholds. AI sees its own forces and the same authorized enemy information as players.
- MIL-01A units in maritime journeys have no regional presence for combat, occupation or fallback. Friendly arrivals and MIL-01C authorized allied arrivals become eligible under their arrival and permission rules. MIL-01B transit supplies location/access constraints without conferring presence for occupation or automatic defence. Revalidate landing, staging and transit permissions when used. Keep PR #39’s separate save design review independent.
- MIL-02 can supply bounded modifiers and resupply effects at the frozen input boundary. MIL-03 can supply naval route and arrival events, escorts, interception and blockades. Mandatory MIL-04 adds hostile coastal arrival, contested combat, configurable landing penalties and a separate temporary beachhead state, with maritime supply, reinforcement and evacuation. Arrival creates an eligible contingent at a beachhead under the rules for that operation. Provincial control changes only through resolved combat and occupation. Future air actions can contribute bounded modifiers or separate arrival events. None of these extensions is required for the first land phase.

## Player experience

- Show a compact **Pending orders** row with the execution week beside the staging action. Show origin, target, own strength and intended persistence in its confirmation. A pending order can be removed with no combat effect.
- Put **Active engagements** first in the Military overview. Show target, friendly committed strength, change this week, a progress label such as **Holding** or **Gaining ground**, and **Reinforce** and **Stage disengagement** actions. Show enemy strength only if authorized, with a range or last-observed week where applicable. If observations are unavailable, say so and omit numerical ratios. Derive any risk band solely from information the viewer is allowed to see. Exact internal progress can disclose hidden force changes, so use coarse, authorized progress observations and test the view for leaks.
- In the detail sheet show each division, remaining personnel and percentage of full strength, last own losses and attempted fallback. State when disengagement settles and what happens if the exit closes. On settlement, clearly distinguish repelled with survivors, retreat destination, pinned, surrendered, captured territory and destruction if later approved. Show only authorized enemy outcomes. Use text plus icons, large touch targets and no color-only states. Link the weekly report to the engagement and return to the overview when the report closes, in portrait and landscape.

## GEO-01 · Unified geographic interface

- Keep the interactive 3D globe after country selection. At high zoom transition smoothly to the detailed 2D regional map. Zooming out reverses the transition. Share a geographic camera center and stable selected region and division IDs between renderers. Use slightly separated entry and exit zoom thresholds to prevent flicker. Verify focus, coordinates and selection across the handoff, including after device rotation. The detailed map remains part of the same geographic view.
- Render division positions only in detailed 2D. Group nearby divisions at wider 2D zoom levels and separate them as zoom increases. Cluster selection can reveal its members for individual or multiple selection. Preserve selection through regrouping. The Military menu supports the same individual and multiple selection. **Locate division** enters detail if needed and centers on an owned division’s known position. A unit without a regional location, such as a future maritime journey, uses its journey status.
- Put **Show orders / Hide orders** directly below the detailed map. Start hidden on entry to a match. Keep division positions visible in 2D in either setting. When enabled distinguish pending orders, active attacks, moves and retreats through labels and line styles as well as color, and show authorized engagement overlays. Global 3D shows no division markers. Filter enemy positions, clusters, orders and overlays by authorized intelligence before rendering. An unseen enemy must not affect cluster counts or overlay geometry.
- Keep geographic transforms and rendering outside the combat engine. Render from authorized read models keyed by region and division IDs. Simulation inputs use scenario graph and order state without reference to camera or zoom.

## Remaining decisions and tuning

Approved initial calibration is 3% base weekly loss, 6% ordinary cap, +15% defender strength, 80% remaining for automatic retreat, progress pace 45, breakthrough +100, and surrender after two consecutive qualifying encirclement weeks. A retreat requires one full recovery week. Retreat and surrender apply symmetrically, prisoners are recorded separately, and exceptional destruction is disabled. Independent morale, equipment and detailed supply are deferred. These settings remain subject to later gameplay balancing.

Review the proposed 25% reciprocal screening threshold, eight-round field clash limit and three-week blocked-route suspension, including cross-capture exposure. The retained baseline is deliberately conservative in the absence of a replenishment mechanic. Review whether depleted formations need an approved recovery mechanism for long matches. Specific fallback ranking, treatment of competing third-party claims, initial intelligence bands and terrain classifications still need playtest or owner decisions. GEO-01 zoom cutoff and clustering distance are implementation tuning.
